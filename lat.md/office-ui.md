# Office UI

The office is a fork of Pixel Agents in `pixel-office/`: the original React canvas, with the crew injected client-side and a Success Manager panel on top.

## Server API

`pixel-office/server/src/sm/smRoutes.ts` exposes `/sm/*` behind the bearer token when `SM_ROOT` is set. Every route calls into [[bin/sm-api.js#createApi]].

`state(name)` in that API builds what the panel shows: crew positions and tasks, missions, questions, reports, ideas and the access flag.

## Webview

The panel lives in `pixel-office/webview-ui/src/sm/`: `SmPanel` (shell), `SmMissions` (mission cards, real changes, replies), `SmInspector` (one person), `SmLiveFeed` (live feed), `SmIdeas` (MUSE) and `SmPromptBar` (talk to ARES).

Crew voice (ElevenLabs): ARES's bubbles and meeting lines are spoken aloud. `bin/voice.js` ([[bin/voice.js#createVoice]]) reads `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` (optional `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID`, `ELEVENLABS_MODEL_ID`) from the root `.env` on every call, so no restart is needed; `/sm/voice` reports on/off and `/sm/voice/speak` returns mp3. The browser side is `sm/voiceOut.ts` (queue of 2, 🔊/🔇 mute button in the prompt bar, hidden while there is no key).

Video voiceover: `sm voiceover "<text>" --out f.mp3` ([[bin/voice.js#createVoice]]) makes ElevenLabs narration with the same key; REEL (remotion-video skill) plays it in Remotion with `<Audio>`, so the key never reaches the worker.

The office phone: [[bin/ares-phone.js#createPhone]] uses the "sms" integration (Twilio). The secretary IVY answers; ARES is no longer the voice. `sm call "<text>"` or POST `/sm/phone/call` rings `owner_phone`; `--whatsapp` texts instead. A small server on 127.0.0.1:4781 (started by the office) is the only public surface Twilio needs: the signature-checked `/ares/whatsapp`, `/ares/voice` (set the number's voice webhook to `<public_url>/ares/voice`) and `/ares/status` webhooks, and the `/ares/relay` WebSocket (one-time token from our own signed TwiML). Two stacks, switched by `phone_stack` in the sms integration: `relay` (default, needs an https `public_url` and the `ws` module) uses Twilio ConversationRelay (Deepgram listening, ElevenLabs speaking, `interruptible=any`), the reply streams in sentence chunks and Twilio stops speaking itself when the caller barges in (the caller's next prompt retires a stale answer; an `interrupt` alone does not); `gather` (fallback, no extra service) uses Polly Neural `<Gather bargeIn>` with one `<Say>` per sentence. Optional sms fields: `relay_voice`, `relay_tts_provider`, `relay_stt_provider`, `gather_voice`, `secretary_name`. The owner talks to IVY's brain ([[bin/ares-chat.js#createChat]]): greetings, thanks and "what did I miss" are answered instantly with no AI call (at most three briefing items); anything else is one [[bin/ares-chat.js#claudeTurn]] (Sonnet, low effort, JSON `{say, action: none|task|note, task, project, note}`; no tools on the phone for speed, about 4s; WhatsApp may read files) with the desk notes, the project list and the call so far. Her style rules: 1-2 short sentences, answer first, never recap or end with "anything else?", act instead of instructing. When the owner asks for work she files it for ARES herself (to the project she picked, else the office) and the call records it as a request; "remember…"/schedule talk becomes a desk note. Cap 25s with "One sec." (not interruptible) after 2.5s. Her own recent words heard back on a speakerphone are ignored as echo. Speech-to-text splits a long sentence into pieces: everything the owner says before her reply is answered as one utterance; a newer piece aborts the older turn's Claude run, and a stale turn files nothing (`stale()`), so one request is filed once. If they stop mid-thought she says only "Mm-hm." and keeps their words. The relay passes crew and project names as Deepgram `hints` (else "ARES" is heard as "Harris"; she is also told the common mishearings). Her context includes the open entries of TRACE's `office/errors.json`. Each call record keeps a `transcript` (both sides, seconds into the call, and her reply time in `ms`), which is where to look first when reviewing a call. A plain "no" ends the call only right after she asked "Anything else?". The owner greeting is one line with at most a count of what waits on them, never the full briefing. Strangers leave a message, are asked their name once if they didn't give it, and learn nothing; silence is asked once then hung up; no caller id is refused. Every call is written to `office/calls.json` by [[bin/call-log.js#createCallLog]] (outcome `owner-chat|request-filed|message-taken|silent|hung-up-early|error|blocked`, caller, summary, requests) and listed by `phone.calls()`. Outbound calls are logged at dial time (`calling`) and closed by the `/ares/status` callback (`no-answer`, `busy`, `failed`, `voicemail` from AnsweredBy, else `delivered`); records also carry the `interrupts` count. IVY greets known numbers by name and her turns get her desk (`knowledge()`, `briefing()`, recent finished/failed items from [[bin/secretary.js#createSecretary]]) in the prompt. "add this to the list …" still files at once without an AI call; said alone she asks what to add and files the next message (disarms after 10 minutes or "never mind"). WhatsApp text replies still wait 10s, then arrive as a normal message.

The secretary's desk: [[bin/secretary.js#createSecretary]] keeps IVY's data in `office/secretary.json` (office tasks the owner hands her, schedule/fact notes, contacts, ticked checklist items) and reads live calls (`phone.calls()`), missions and owner questions through deps. It builds the "missed while away" checklist (stranger calls and messages, finished or failed missions, questions needing the owner, due tasks; ticks persist), the callback queue (strangers who left a message, went silent or hit an error), caller recognition by contact number (`recognise(number)`, for the engine to greet by name), a spoken `briefing()` and a text `digest()` that `sendDigest` sends over WhatsApp. Routes are `GET /sm/secretary` and `POST /sm/secretary/<action>` (the body `{id, done}` is accepted wherever the CLI passes `(id, …)`); the UI is the IVY tab (`sm/SmSecretary.tsx`: AWAY, TASKS, CALLS, KNOWS) and the same desk as a window (`SmSecretaryModal`). Owner hands her work with `addTask` (`{title, detail, due, project, toAres}`): by default (`toAres` true) she passes it on to ARES as a mission through the `handOff` dep (`M.create`, default project success-manager) and the task keeps `mission {project,id}`; `state()` adds its live `missionStatus`. Untick "Pass it on to ARES" for a plain reminder.

Reception (`office-layout/generate.js`, cols 46-57, rows 21-31, door from the Studio floor) is IVY's office: room `reception` in [[bin/staff.js#createStaff]] (she moves there from Marketing once, when the room first appears), seat `sm-seat-secretary`. She always sits at her desk (`useSuccessManager` forces her WORKING: no lounge, dancing, cheering, blocked-pose or idle wandering; meetings do not move her either). Zoo animals stay inside the Zoo enclosure (cols 1-45, rows 22-30, `inZoo` in `petEntity.ts`): spawn, wander targets and whole paths are checked, and an escaped one is put back. Clickable: the RECEPTION sign opens her desk; `SmOverlay` `IVY_SPOTS` hang ☎ calls / 📝 tasks / 🗂 away / 📇 knows buttons on furniture uids `sm-rec-desk|board|files|shelf`; ☎ and 📝 chips float over IVY herself; her inspector card has the same four buttons. Not yet wired: the engine does not call `recognise` to greet by name or feed `briefing()` to her answer prompt. Unverified by any real phone call; relay cost (~$0.08/min) and whether ElevenLabs/Deepgram are bundled are unconfirmed.

Product demo (app renamed Throngs, logo by Igani in `SmDemo.tsx` `Logo`, zoomed live screenshots in `public-sm/`): `videos/src/SmDemo.tsx` (composition `SmDemo`, 122s) uses office screenshots + per-scene ElevenLabs narration from `videos/scripts/sm-demo-script.json` (mp3s in `videos/public-sm/vo/`). Render with `--public-dir=public-sm`; if Remotion's own Chrome hangs, pass `--browser-executable` pointing at the Playwright `chrome-headless-shell`. Output: `videos/out/throngs-demo.mp4` (copied to success-manager-demo.mp4).

Hype demo: `videos/src/SmHype.tsx` (composition `SmHype`, ~90s) is a fast-cut version with five ElevenLabs voices (Josh, Rachel, Adam, Bella, Antoni) as named speakers. Script + voice per line in `videos/scripts/hype-script.json`, mp3s in `videos/public-sm/hype/`, output `videos/out/throngs-hype-demo.mp4`. Remotion's bundled Chrome crashes here ("Target closed"): pass `--browser-executable` for the installed Google Chrome.

Voice: the 🎤 button in `SmPromptBar` uses browser speech recognition (`sm/useVoice.ts`), sends the transcript to ARES when you stop talking, and publishes mic loudness via `sm/voiceLevel.ts`; while you speak every crew member stops, faces the camera and trembles (2D renderer and 3D view).

Drag crew: press and hold on a person in the 2D office and move more than 5px to lift them (they shake in fear, `sm/dragState.ts`); letting go drops them on the nearest walkable tile, they tip over, lie a beat, then get up and carry on. `OfficeCanvas` handles input, `updateCharacter` freezes them while `isDragged`, `renderer.ts` draws the pose. 2D view only.

Every agent message input (prompt bar, mission composer, answer and reply boxes, inspector task box) accepts pasted, dropped or picked images through `useImageAttach`, which uploads via `/sm/upload` and appends the saved paths with `withImages`; thumbnails come from `ImageStrip`.

Crew characters use ids from 990000 and are driven by `transport.deliver()` messages. Colours must come from CSS variables, because the repo lint forbids inline colours.

## Onboarding, install and Firebase

First launch shows `sm/SmOnboarding.tsx`; the `? TOUR` button replays it.

Steps: welcome, intro video, feature cards, setup check, go. State lives in `office/onboarding/state.json` ([[bin/onboarding.js#createOnboarding]], routes `/sm/onboarding*`); the video is the compressed `office/onboarding/throngs-demo.mp4` (6 MB copy of `videos/out/throngs-demo.mp4`, re-encode with ffmpeg when the demo changes).

`install.sh` / `start.sh` install and launch on a new machine; `sm doctor` checks Node, Claude CLI, build and `.env`. Firebase: [[bin/firebase.js#createFirebase]] pushes/pulls an encrypted (scrypt + AES-256-GCM) copy of `office/integrations.json` to Firestore `sm_backups/default` with a service-account key stored in `office/firebase-link.json` (`sm firebase link|push|pull|unlink`, `/sm/firebase*`). The Zoo has a "Firebase backup" card (`FirebaseCard` in `SmRoom.tsx`) to link a pasted key, back up and restore with a passphrase. Linked to project your-firebase-project and round-tripped against real Firestore.

Google sign-in needs no codes: with Firebase linked, [[bin/integrations.js#createIntegrations]] takes the client ID and secret of the Google app Firebase made (`googleClient` in [[bin/firebase.js#createFirebase]]). Before sending the owner to Google, it checks that Google accepts the office's return address. If not, it answers 409 with a `fixUrl` to that app's settings, shown as a link on the card.

## Health board

The HEALTH tab (`SmHealth.tsx`) shows one card per project, most urgent first, from `/sm/health`. [[bin/health.js#createHealth]] reads LATEST.md, history.log and task priorities.

Rank: GAVE UP, QA failing, P0/P1 open, then the rest. `/api/health` serves the stand-alone dashboard.

## Layout

The office floor plan is generated by `office-layout/generate.js` into `office-layout/layout.json` and installed to `~/.pixel-agents/layout.json` with a backup.

In the Layout editor, the "Grow office" buttons (`handleExpandOffice` in `useEditorActions.ts`) add 4 empty tiles on any side (max 96x96) so there is room to paint new rooms. `webview-ui/scripts/gen-furniture.cjs` generates 24 extra furniture sprites (chairs, storage, tech, decor, tables, wall items) into `public/assets/furniture/`. Every non-wall piece is a rotation group (front keeps the plain id, a back view = front flipped and darker, and an upright side profile (never the picture lying on its side) with a mirrored other side), so R rotates it in the editor.

## Smoke test

[[bin/office-smoke.js]] calls the API for every project and fails on any error. It is part of [[bin/missions.js#OFFICE_QA]].

## Agent looks

Each agent's look is the baked base sprite plus optional layers: hair, outfit, hat, accessory and colour swatches, drawn at runtime by `pixel-office/webview-ui/src/office/sprites/layers.ts`.

Option ids live in `lookOptions.json`. The optional `look` object on a staff record is validated against those ids by [[bin/staff.js#sanitizeLook]] (unknown values dropped, defaults mean no change). The customizer with live preview is in `sm/SmAgents.tsx`.

## 3D office

`pixel-office/webview-ui/src/office3d/` is a bundled three.js first-person walk-through built from the same layout and agents as the 2D office.

`layout3d.ts` converts the layout to metres and handles collision, `agents3d.ts` merges characters with crew data, `scene3d.ts` renders and `Office3D.tsx` adds the chat panel. It is Minecraft-style, built from the 2D pixel art: every object is a real 3D voxel model, one voxel per sprite pixel (16 per tile). `models3d.ts` spins round things (bin, cup, plants), with off-axis bits such as a cup handle kept as a thin piece. People are carved from their front, back and side drawings, each face showing the drawing that looks at it, and they turn the way they walk. Furniture (desks, tables, sofas, chairs, benches, PCs, shelves, whiteboards) is built from parts in the sprite's own colours, with screens, books and boards showing the sprite. Side-facing variants are rotated. Wall art hangs on the room side of the wall tile at the bottom of its footprint. `pixelVoxels.ts` is the flat fallback. Meshes are cached per sprite and model. A tab left open across a rebuild reloads once instead of failing to load the 3D code. F toggles creative flying: Space and C move up and down, and above the walls you fly over them with the ceiling hidden. `pixelTextures.ts` bakes the floor tiles (with seams) and brick walls. Monitors show each agent's live task; chat files a mission "For NAME (role): …" through `smClient`.

The 3D HUD is pixel-styled DOM over the canvas: `Office3D.tsx` holds the Back-to-2D and Reports buttons, a framed minimap and a controls hint that fades after you start looking around (`hud3d.ts` rules). `WorldBubbles.tsx` pins Success Manager speech and "Need your help!" bubbles over characters by projecting their head position through the scene camera each frame (data from `smBubbles.ts`). `Office3DLayer` sits above the 2D `SmOverlay` so its 2D-positioned banners never show in 3D.

The 3D office is interactive: Enter/T or the PROMPT button opens a box that files a mission with ARES (same path as chat). Tab frees the mouse so you can click; where the webview refuses pointer lock (the Mac app) `scene3d.ts` sets `lockFailed` and falls back to drag-to-look (pointer travel when `movementX` is missing) and click-to-pick at the pointer; clicking a person (crosshair when captured, pointer when free) opens their chat, other people, animals and furniture show a name toast (`OfficeScene.pickAt` raycasts, `onPick`).

Crew rules in 2D (and so 3D, which reads the same characters): only working agents sit at a computer, and only at their own desk (a working crew member with no desk, e.g. in a restricted room, stands or rests in the lounge instead of typing or wandering in the corridor; idle ones stand up and wander or rest on the lounge sofas), nobody shares a tile (`officeState.update` blocks everyone else's tile for each walker; `characters.ts` waits then re-routes), and room signs and zoo name tags sit at z-5, under the zoom, toolbar and prompt controls.

The 3D view shows an "IN <room>" chip under the Back-to-2D buttons (it has no wall signs) and its controls hint is width-capped so it never runs into the PROMPT button.

## Conference Room

Top right of the floor plan (cols 36-46, rows 0-9): a long table with chairs `sm-seat-conf-1..10` and the host's head chair `sm-seat-conf-host` facing a wall TV. Meetings are stored per project in `projects/<name>/meetings.json` by [[bin/meetings.js#createMeetings]].

A meeting is created when a mission finishes a video (see [[missions#Videos and meetings]]) or by hand with `sm meeting <project> "<title>" [--video f.mp4] [--say ".."]`. While one is `calling` or `in-meeting`, `useSuccessManager` seats the whole crew round the table (`sm/meeting.ts` `meetingSeats`: host at the head, the video's maker first). `SmMeeting.tsx` shows a ringing "Conference Room is calling you" banner; Join zooms into the meeting, where ARES's lines (`briefing`) explain what was done, then the video plays with Start, Pause and Stop. End meeting marks it `ended` and the crew go back to their desks.

The meeting room (`SmMeeting.tsx`) is a wide window: a three.js conference room (`MeetingRoom3D.tsx`: table, crew in chairs, ARES by a wall monitor that plays the video through a `VideoTexture`; Flat view swaps to a plain player) beside a sidebar with ARES's avatar (`CharacterMugShot`) and speech bubbles, and the comments box.

Comments have a clear Notes | Questions switch ([[bin/meetings.js#createMeetings]] `addNote`). A question goes to ARES at once as a mission (answer only). A note is only held on the meeting; Eject (End) makes [[bin/sm-api.js#createApi]] start one mission for ARES with the whole list (`takeHeld` hands them out once). "Show in folder" calls `reveal`, which runs `open -R` on the recorded file.

A meeting can hold a `pdf` instead of a video ([[bin/meetings.js#pdfFile]], `GET /sm/meeting/pdf`): the room shows the PDF in a flat viewer (blob URL in an iframe) after ARES walks through FOLIO's summary and points.

Routes: `POST /sm/meeting/join`, `POST /sm/meeting/end`, `POST /sm/meeting/comment`, `POST /sm/meeting/note/drop`, `POST /sm/meeting/reveal`, `GET /sm/meeting/video` (streams only the file recorded on the meeting; the client fetches it with the bearer token into a blob URL).

The call banner only shows while the office dropdown is on the meeting's project. A video mission also waits in the queue (one mission per project) until the running one ends. Live-tested 2026-10-28: `sm meeting` call, Join, ARES briefing, video player loaded, End all work; a real 5s Remotion render (`videos/`) was played in the live player: currentTime advanced 1.4 → 3.3 → 5.0 s, then it paused at the end. Nothing in the engine or UI ends a meeting by itself; only End (or `sm meeting --end`) does, so an early end means something clicked End.

New rooms reach an owner-edited office through `office-layout/merge.js` ([[office-layout/merge.js#mergeLayout]]): it only fills empty tiles, opens doors next to the new floor and adds named (`sm-*`, not `sm-f-N`) furniture, so grown floors and moved furniture survive.

## Zoo

The Zoo is a room below the office (rows 22-30 of the layout) where ten animals live. Each animal is one linked integration (WhatsApp lion, Google zebra, Teams elephant, Notion, Telegram, Slack, Zoom, Dropbox, SMS, Discord), not an AI.

[[bin/integrations.js#createIntegrations]] holds the catalog and the owner's keys in `office/integrations.json` (gitignored, mode 600). The Zoo room dialog in `SmRoom.tsx` links, saves and unlinks each one through `/sm/integrations*`; secrets only come back masked. Engineers and ARES get a prompt line from `promptLine` ([[bin/staff.js#createStaff]] `promptFor`) naming the linked services, and read real keys with `sm integration get <id>`, so "send this to Dana" can use the owner's Gmail or WhatsApp. `sm integration test [id]` live-tests linked services (`check` in [[bin/integrations.js#createIntegrations]]): Google exchanges its refresh token, others only report saved keys.

Where a service supports it (Google, Teams, Slack, Notion, Zoom, Dropbox) the card has a **Sign in** button instead of pasted codes: the owner saves that service's OAuth app client ID/secret once, then [[bin/integrations.js#createIntegrations]] `oauthStart` builds the consent URL and `oauthCallback` (route `/sm/oauth/callback`, one-time state, no token) stores the returned tokens. Each card shows the company logo as 16x16 pixel art from `PixelLogo.tsx`.

[[office-layout/generate.js]] draws the room and places pets `sm-zoo-0..9` (`office-layout/merge.js` adds missing ones to an owner-edited layout). [[pixel-office/webview-ui/src/office/sprites/zooAnimals.ts#zooPetTemplates]] draws their sprites in code, in the catalog's `animal` order, and `setPetTemplates` appends them after the bundled pets. `isZooPet` in `petEntity.ts` stops them following people and keeps their wandering and spawning on rows from `ZOO_FIRST_ROW`. The 3D office shows them too: `buildPets3D` feeds `OfficeScene.setPets`, which turns each animal's side-view sprite into a voxel model with a name label. `SmOverlay.tsx` draws a name tag over each animal once zoomed in, and the room is dressed with hedges, rocks and trees.

Clips come from the Thronglets game in `public/assets/throng`. The inspector shows the throng card with ▶ voice / ♪ / 🍎.

## Studio

The Studio (`office-layout/generate.js`, cols 46-57 at the hallway's east end) is the music room.

🎵 MUSIC (`setMusicOn` in `sm/throngStudio.ts`) sends every idle throng there: `useSuccessManager` gives each a dance spot from the current choreography (`formationSpots` over `studioPool`; `FORMATIONS`: circle, lines, rings, V, diamond, spiral, columns, changing every `FORMATION_BARS` bars via `formationIndex`) and `smPose: 'dance'`; `characters.ts` walks them to the spot and dances on the beat only once they stand on it (`isDancingNow`; on the way they just walk) (`SM_DANCE_BPM`). The song (`startSong`, `songBar`) is built from their own clips: synth beat and bass on an I–V–vi–IV loop, one throng singing its part each bar (catchphrase as bubble lyric, pitch snapped to semitones), a purr answering, and a group chorus every 4th bar. Throngs on the floor or walking to it sing; clips are preloaded (`preloadSong`) when music starts. `merge.js` grows the owner's live grid when a generated room lies past its edge.

## Dev pulse and IVY's day

The owner's day as a developer, with no AI: every repo and GitHub ranked by what needs them ([[bin/devpulse.js#createDevPulse]]), plus IVY's reminders that fire and a morning brief on WhatsApp.

[[bin/devpulse.js#createDevPulse]] scans every git repo directly in the projects folder (and one level down): branch, uncommitted and untracked files, ahead/behind the upstream, last commit, commits in the last 24 hours. One `gh api graphql` call adds, for the owner's 30 most recently pushed GitHub repos, the default branch's check state (failing check names), open PRs and issues, open security alerts, plus their own open PRs (checks, review decision, mergeable), review requests and assigned issues. All git/gh calls are async and in parallel (about 3.5s), so the office server never stalls; the cache is `office/devpulse.json` (personal, gitignored) and `current()` returns it synchronously, rescanning in the background after 15 minutes. `attention()` ranks items 0-3: failing checks and security alerts first, then review requests, your PRs (failing, approved and mergeable, changes requested), unpushed commits older than a day, uncommitted work, being behind. Repos untouched for 45+ days drop to level 3 (mentioned, never nagged), and a repo owned by someone else (e.g. pixel-office's upstream) never counts as unpushed. `forProject(dir)` is the Repo state block the mission Planner and ARES get in their planning prompts ([[missions#Planning]]): branch, uncommitted work, recent commits and CI, so a plan builds on what is really there. `sm pulse [--refresh] [--all] [--json]` prints it; `GET /sm/pulse` (POST rescans) serves it.

IVY's day ([[bin/sm-api.js#createApi]] `ivyDay`, started by the office server, ticks every 30s): reminders on her desk ([[bin/secretary.js#createSecretary]] `addReminder({text, at, via})`, future times only, `whatsapp` or `call`) are sent when due and marked sent or failed (failures go to TRACE); the morning brief (`briefTime`, default 08:30, `briefOn`) goes out on WhatsApp once per local day between briefTime and noon: today's calendar events and reminders, what needs the owner, the code items (attention levels 0-2), missions that finished or failed in the last 24h, and what they shipped yesterday. `morningBrief()` also returns a spoken version, which is what she reads when the owner asks "what's my day look like" (an instant reply, no AI). On the phone and WhatsApp she can set reminders herself (AI action `remind`, with an ISO time; her prompt carries the local date, time and UTC offset), and her context includes the dev pulse. `sm today [--send]` prints or sends the brief; `sm remind "<text>" --at <HH:MM|ISO> [--call] | --list | --cancel <id>`. In the office, Reception opens IVY's desk on the TODAY tab: the brief preview, the daily-send time and switch, "Send it now", reminders (add with a date-time and an optional call), and the code list with Rescan. `sm office --check` verifies the pulse, the desk and the brief on the live server.

## IVY on Telegram

Telegram (the Zoo's "telegram" integration, bot @throngsIvy_bot) is IVY's main text line: she tells the owner about finished/failed missions and blocked teammates, their replies answer the office, and an unanswered urgent question makes her call.

[[bin/telegram.js#createTelegram]] is the transport: long polling (`getUpdates`, no public URL), the update offset saved in `office/telegram.json` (personal, gitignored) so a restart never replays messages, plain text, only `default_chat_id` (the owner) reaches her and anyone else gets "This is a private line". [[bin/ivy-telegram.js#createIvyTelegram]] is the gateway, started with IVY's day by the office server: every 30s it sends each newly finished or failed mission (headline, summary, "For you" unless nothing) and each new teammate question (who, project, what, why, steps, what to reply), once each; the first run only records what already exists. A reply to a question message is that teammate's answer (`M.answer`); a reply to a report is a follow-up for ARES (`M.reply`); any other text is a conversation with Ivy through the same brain as the phone (`phone.converse`, context includes the dev pulse), where she can also answer an open question herself (AI action `answer` with the question's `project/mission/subtask` ref, listed in her prompt as "Open questions from the team"). Buttons: a persistent button bar (Today, Waiting on me, Status, Code, Open folder, Where, Urgent, Help; sent with /start, /help, /menu and `sm telegram --menu`) runs the matching command; the "/" menu is registered with `setMyCommands` when the gateway starts. A question message carries its answers as buttons when the agent's `reply` is a short choice ("A or B", 2-4 options of up to 30 chars) plus "Answer…" (a force-reply prompt whose reply maps back to the same question); a tapped answer is sent to the agent and the buttons change to "Answered: …". Reports carry "Follow up" (force-reply for ARES) and "Thanks". /open with no folder shows a folder picker (the office's projects first). /waiting re-sends every open question with its buttons. /today, /code, /status, /where, /urgent, /help are instant. Urgent = a teammate blocked on the owner: if neither a reply nor an answer elsewhere arrives within `escalateMin` (default 10) she phones once (`phone.call`, relay stack so the owner can answer on the call) and notes it on Telegram; `quiet` hours and `callOnUrgent` switch it off (`sm telegram --escalate <min> --quiet HH:MM-HH:MM --no-call`). When Telegram is linked, the morning brief, reminders and the digest go there instead of WhatsApp. `sm telegram --test` sends a test message.

The office's focus (which folder the team works in; home stays success-manager) is shared state in `office/focus.json` ([[bin/sm-api.js#createApi]] `focus`): the UI writes it when the owner picks a project (`POST /sm/focus`) and polls it every 4s, switching when someone else (IVY) moved it. IVY moves it from Telegram (`/open <folder>`, `/where`) or in conversation (AI action `switch`): `focus.open(query)` matches a managed project by name (case, spaces and dashes ignored, "the … folder" stripped), else a folder in the projects folder (exact, then contains), adding it to the office first, else a `~/` or absolute path inside home. Her prompt lists the current folder and the projects folder's subfolders, and a task she files without a project goes to the current folder.

## Phone line address

Twilio reaches the phone server (127.0.0.1:4781) through a Cloudflare quick tunnel, whose address dies when its connection drops (sleep, network change) and changes on every restart; [[bin/tunnel.js#createTunnel]] keeps it alive.

Started with IVY's day by the office server: it runs cloudflared detached (`--metrics 127.0.0.1:20242`, so an office restart keeps the address; pid in `office/tunnel.json`), checks cloudflared's `/ready` and `/quicktunnel` every minute, restarts it after two checks with no connection, and whenever the address differs it saves `public_url` in the sms integration and sets the Twilio number's VoiceUrl (and StatusCallback) to the new address, then tells the owner on Telegram. It only ever stops the pid it started, and only while that pid is still cloudflared. `sm tunnel` shows the line; `sm tunnel --fix` checks and repairs it now. The same sync sets the number's VoiceFallbackUrl to a voicemail served by Twilio's echo twimlet ([[bin/tunnel.js#FALLBACK_URL]]), so when the office can't be reached at all Twilio still answers and records a message. [[bin/missed-calls.js#createMissedCalls]] (every 2 minutes) lists Twilio's recent inbound calls; one the call log doesn't have is logged as `missed` or `voicemail-left` and sent to the owner on Telegram, the recording as audio. `sm office --check` checks the line end to end: tunnel connected, Twilio pointing at it, fallback on, the public address reaching the office.

Calls: a relay call carries `<Connect action=…/ares/voice?after=relay>`; if the live stream drops while the caller is still on, Twilio asks that URL and IVY carries on with the gather stack ("Sorry, I lost you for a second…", at most twice), and when she ended the call herself it just hangs up (signatures cover the full URL with its query). A short reply made only of closing words ("No. That's it. Thank you.") right after "Anything else?" ends the call. When a caller leaves a message the owner gets it on Telegram as soon as the call ends (`onCallEnded`), with the number to call back. Contact names are capitalised in greetings. Learned 2026-10-30: a hand-started quick tunnel lost its connection overnight and every call came back busy/no-answer at Twilio without reaching the office.

## Caller scenarios

Callers who aren't the owner get their own brain ([[bin/ares-chat.js#callerTurn]]): no tools, no office context, run outside the office folder, a JSON turn `{say, intent, callerName, company, contact, details, bestTime, notifyNow, transfer, end}`.

Intents: message, callback, lead, delivery, urgent, personal, availability, spam, wrong_number, language, abusive, other. Privacy is absolute (nothing about the owner's whereabouts, schedule, address, clients or work), the owner is named rather than gendered, and replies are English only (non-English or garbled speech → `language`). [[bin/ares-phone.js#createPhone]] applies the turn: `details`/lead fields are kept on the call (and in the record, with `intent`), `notifyNow` calls `onUrgent` once (Telegram mid-call), `transfer` puts the live call through when `canTransfer` allows (policy `sm phone --transfer contacts|urgent|never`, default known contacts + urgent, never in Telegram quiet hours; refused → the owner is alerted instead and she says she can't), `end` ends the call. A put-through replaces the call's TwiML with a `<Dial timeout=20 action=…?after=dial>` (REST update on relay, inline on gather); an answered dial ends as `transferred`, otherwise IVY is back on the line taking the message. If the caller brain fails, the deterministic message-taker runs. Outcomes add `transferred`, `spam`, `wrong-number`, `abusive`; spam/wrong-number/abusive are logged without an alert, every other caller ends with a labelled Telegram message (🆕 New lead, 📦 Delivery, 🚨 Urgent, 💬 Personal, 📞 Call back…) with the details, best time and number. A number that called in the last 12 hours is greeted "again". The owner calling from another phone says "it's <name>" and gives the PIN (spoken digits; `sm phone --pin`, stored as a salted SHA-256 in `office/secretary.json`) to switch to the owner brain; two wrong tries keep them a caller. The owner can say "connect me to <contact>" (owner action `connect`, `findContact`); by text she only gives the number.

## Voice speed and sound

The owner's calls run on one warm streaming session ([[bin/voice-brain.js#createVoiceSession]]): `claude -p` in stream-json mode with partial messages, opened when the call starts (it warms while the greeting plays) and closed when it ends.

Each finished sentence is spoken at once while the rest is written; measured 2026-11-01 first words in ~0.8-1.4s on Haiku versus ~4-5s for a cold turn. The system prompt ([[bin/ares-chat.js#voiceSystem]]) is the owner brain's rules plus spoken-style rules (a short first sentence, everyday words, times said as people say them) and the reply format: spoken text, then optionally one never-spoken line `ACTION: {json}` with the same fields as the cold turn's schema. [[bin/ares-chat.js#createChat]] uses the session when the call has one (falls back to a cold turn if it fails), keeps the streamed words as the reply for actions the model already announced, and returns a correction or a lookup's real answer as extra words, which the relay adds after what was streamed. Turns queue on the session; a stale one (the owner kept talking) is drained without being spoken, and the merged words are sent marked as their whole message. The model is `sm phone --model haiku|sonnet` (default haiku). The relay uses Deepgram Flux (`speechModel="flux"`, `eotThreshold` 0.75, `speechTimeout` 1500 as the maximum silence) for turn detection, and ElevenLabs v4 (`<voice>-v4-0.4_0.8`) for the voice; `relay_voice`, `relay_stt_model` and `relay_eot_threshold` in the sms integration override them. Measured on the first real call (2026-11-01): 2-5s to first words because every turn rebuilt the desk notes (every project's state, beads included, ~2.7s); now a warm turn reads nothing (the context, briefing and today are getters, the chat's project/question lists are lazy, `openQuestions`/`allMissions` are cached 20s) and the session opens right after the TwiML is sent. Relay also sends `partialPrompts` so the 30s silence timer resets while the owner is still talking (it once asked "Are you still there?" mid-sentence); a goodbye ends the call even with half-heard words pending, and the brain can end it (action `hangup`). Her context includes the crew and the Claude accounts METER switches between (`officeInfo`). Second real call: 2.1-2.9s to first words; traced to Claude's time-to-first-token with a ~13k-character system prompt (~2s on the first turn while the prompt cache is written, ~1.2-1.4s after; no thinking blocks). So the session sends one silent warm-up turn as it opens (the cache is written during the greeting), and a short varied filler ("Mm.", "Okay.", "Let's see."…, never the same twice) plays after 1s instead of "One sec." after 2.5s.

## IVY and Google

IVY works in the owner's Google account through [[bin/google.js#createGoogle]]: calendar, free/busy, Meet meetings, Gmail sends and contact lookup.

The account is the Zoo's google integration (the owner's Google account; granted Gmail, Calendar, Contacts, Drive).

Meetings go on the primary calendar with `conferenceData` (hangoutsMeet) and `sendUpdates=all`, so Google itself emails each guest the invitation with the Meet link and accept buttons. Owner actions `meet` (with, title, start ISO with offset, minutes, body) and `email` (with, title = subject, body) never send directly: [[bin/ares-chat.js#createChat]] resolves the people (spoken addresses like "dana at acme dot co", else Google Contacts), checks free/busy, reads the details back ("A 30-minute Google Meet with Dana Cole, Tuesday 3 November at 10am. You already have something then. Send the invite?") and keeps a draft per conversation thread (`call:<sid>` or the text line) for 10 minutes; only a yes on the same thread sends it, a no cancels it, anything else drops it. Works on calls and on Telegram. The office shows the calendar on IVY's desk (CALENDAR tab, the 📅 spot in Reception, the 📅 button on IVY's card): a Sunday-first week agenda across the calendars with Join (Meet) and open-in-Google buttons, and a "+ Meet" form (title, guests, date, time, length) that creates the meeting and sends the invites. Routes: `GET /sm/calendar?from&to`, `POST /sm/calendar/meet`. Live-checked 2026-11-01: reading events, and creating a Meet link (a guest-less test event, deleted at once).

Addresses said out loud are read by code, not the model ([[bin/spoken-email.js#spokenEmail]]): a spelled run of letters ("a l e x r i v e r a at g mail dot com") wins over words, the last address said wins, "g mail"/"dot"/"underscore" are understood. On the first real send (2026-11-01) the model joined the spelled letters into alexriveras@gmail.com, read it back as one word, and the invite bounced; now an unknown name puts the draft in `needAddress` and the owner's next words are parsed directly, spoken addresses are spelled back letter by letter ([[bin/spoken-email.js#spellEmail]]), saying a different address fixes the draft, and a call's draft is mirrored to Telegram (exact address, Send / Cancel buttons `gs:`/`gx:`, a reply = a corrected address; `confirmDraft(id)`). After a send, named people are remembered on IVY's contacts with their email (`rememberEmail`; next time the name is enough and comes first, before Google Contacts), and Gmail is checked for a mailer-daemon bounce at 90s and 6 minutes (`google.bounced`): a bounce is reported on Telegram and the address is forgotten.

## Ivy calls for the owner

The owner can have IVY phone someone ("call Dana and tell her I'm ten minutes late", "call the dentist and confirm my Tuesday 3pm"): owner action `callout` (contact or number, message in third person, goal deliver|confirm|ask).

[[bin/ares-chat.js#createChat]] reads it back ("I'll call Dana Cole and say: … Call now?", with the local time if it's before 8 or after 9pm) and dials only on a yes in the same thread; a number said out loud is read by code ([[bin/spoken-email.js#spokenPhone]], read back digit by digit). [[bin/ares-phone.js#createPhone]] `callOut` converts local numbers with the owner's country code, dials over the relay with "Hi Dana, this is Ivy, Alex's assistant." and `MachineDetection=DetectMessageEnd`; a warm delegate session ([[bin/ares-chat.js#delegateSystem]]: one job, nothing else about the owner, never gendered, ACTION line with outcome delivered|confirmed|declined|rescheduled|answered|callback|wrong_person|unclear, result and end) runs the conversation. On their voicemail (AnsweredBy `machine_end_*`) the call's TwiML is replaced with the message and a hang-up. Outcomes `callout-<outcome>`, `voicemail-left`, or Twilio's no-answer/busy; the owner gets a Telegram report ("📞 Called Dana — delivered. Dana is fine; she'll wait by the window."). Sessions are protected against stalls ([[bin/voice-brain.js#createVoiceSession]]: no first words in 9s or no end in 25s shuts the session; a 464s stall was seen in testing): the owner's turn falls back to a cold turn, a delegate call reopens its session once and otherwise apologises and ends.
